## Understanding Batch Auctions

<div class="stub">

- Audience: knows CLOBs and trading generally; may not know batch auctions
- CLOB baseline in one paragraph: continuous matching, price-time priority, being first is worth money
- Sniping / latency arbitrage, told properly: stale quote, fastest sniper wins, maker eats the loss and widens to cover it, the market pays the spread. The arms race is a structural tax on takers (Budish et al.)
- Frequent batch auctions (Budish): discretize time, seal the window, clear at one uniform price; speed stops being a weapon, competition moves to price
- Dual-flow batch auctions (Jump Crypto, 2025): maker and taker as intent-declared flows; two simultaneous auctions per ~100ms window (maker bids vs taker sells, maker asks vs taker buys), each clearing at the single price that maximizes matched volume, pro-rata at ties; makers never trade with makers
- DFBA vs slow batching: ~100ms to 1s cadence is imperceptible to humans (~250ms reaction time) while still killing the race
- Landing: whichever variant, at every seal the venue holds something a CLOB structurally cannot: a complete, committed picture of the market

</div>

## How fees work today

<div class="stub">

- Status quo: maker/taker splits, volume tiers, rebates. Volume is not laziness: it is close to the only thing a continuous venue can price. No instant of coherent market state exists; quotes flash and cancel; roles are assigned by arrival; the only thing that accumulates verifiably is your executed tape
- Side effects worth naming: new entrants pay full freight until they build history; tiers compound toward incumbents and crowd out the long tail
- The seal changes what is knowable: every order committed, blind, simultaneous. Nobody can pose in it retroactively, since anything displayed was genuinely fillable that window. For the first time there is a coherent object to price besides the tape
- Same natural flow, smaller number: a batch venue prints less volume than a CLOB for identical natural trading, because the latency-arb volume never happens (and what is missing is disproportionately the volume nobody wanted to reward). Volume tiers would still work here, just recalibrated
- But rescaling admits the number was a proxy. Volume proxies a participant's relative value to the market (conflating liquidity brought with flow brought). The question writes itself: could that value be assessed directly, in the auction itself, instead of through the accumulated proxy?

</div>

## Why price the snapshot

<div class="stub">

- Batch venues are young; the fee question is genuinely open. Existing designs reach for familiar shapes (flat taker bps, makers free; the DFBA paper sketches three such options in a paragraph). None yet price the snapshot. Before conventions calcify: what should fees on these venues be?
- The proposal in one line: compute each participant's fee from the sealed market state, charging for what their orders are doing for the market in this window
- Stakes, framed as open questions: would spreads tighten? would makers compete on the present quality of their liquidity instead of accumulated history? does that lower the bar for new entrants and make long-tail markets quotable?
- Why crypto first: no entrenched designated-maker agreements or negotiated rebate deals to displace (tradfi maker relationships are bespoke contracts, a different world); fees already quoted in bps; venues young enough to adopt new structure
- Pivot: the DFBA paper's third fee sketch is maker fees as a fixed fraction of distance from the midpoint, one sentence, left hanging. This piece pulls that thread

</div>

## The idea: directionality is the price

<div class="stub">

- Replace maker/taker with a spectrum of directionality
- Confidently two-sided near fair value: trades free. Fully one-sided: pays the taker rate
- A one-sided resting order is a patient taker
- Everything is computed fresh from one sealed snapshot per window: memoryless, verifiable, no tiers, no identity

</div>

## The Base Fee: Pricing Distance from the Mark

The base fee prices a single order by one measure: how far it stands from the
market's Mark Price: `M`.

```
d = bps beyond the band edge (0 if inside the band)

base fee(d) = min(F, k₁ × min(d, Z) + k₂ × max(0, d − Z))
```

- `M`: the Mark price; a more accurate midpoint for the market. Liquidity standing in the inner band and Maker Zone contributes to its calculation. how comes later.
- `B`: the Inner Band: the region `M ± B/2`, declared by the market. The base fee there is zero, so `d` starts counting at the band edge, `B/2` from `M`.
- `Z`, the Maker Zone: the working radius past the band edge. Inside it, distance is priced gently.
- `k₁`, the zone slope: fee per bps of distance inside the zone.
- `k₂`, the far slope: takes over past the zone edge and runs the fee to
  the cap.
- `F`, the fee cap: the taker rate. No resting order ever pays more than a taker.

<div class="stub">

- Net edge: distance earns 1:1 while the schedule takes its slope per bps; `k₁` below 1 keeps width profitable
- Full-fee point: once the fee reaches `F`, the schedule stops caring

</div>

## The Mark is communal

<div class="stub">

- In a real market M is measured from everyone's book at once
- Eligibility: only demonstrated two-sided size near the touch gets a vote
- Your voice is your share of the measuring walk
- Confident non-directionality toward the mid is a vote for M, and tighter quoting carries more weight
- Your fees come from your book, but judged against a yardstick you only partly control
- Needs its own beat: what happens when there is not enough stock to compute M

## Which auction design fits best

<div class="stub">

- Placement TBD (likely here). The intro stays neutral on FBA vs DFBA; this section carries the argument
- DFBA sends taker buys and sells to separate auctions: they never net against each other, so gross urgency walks the maker book. That is the venue type the gentle stamp slope is calibrated for; in netting venues deep excursions collapse and the calibration must be refit
- The maker flow is an explicit standing book: exactly the object the snapshot reads
- Pro-rata at equal prices in DFBA matching rhymes with the attribution-free, pro-rata mark walk: a shared fairness grammar
- Declared roles vs measured behavior: DFBA makes maker/taker an explicit declaration; snapshot fees reads directionality from the book. It can price within the declared maker flow, or dissolve the declaration entirely
- Plain FBA: the formula ports verbatim (roles never appear in it); the calibration does not, and should be fit to the venue's sweep-depth distribution

</div>

## Concerns, variations, open questions

<div class="stub">

- Semi-far coverage earns a discount: paid optionality, priced by fill risk and adverse selection
- Manipulation economics: junk coverage is worthless, bending M requires standing real paired size
- Parameters and governance: B declared and sticky, F venue-wide, Z and the two slopes shape the curve, T measured from demand
- Variations considered: partner-stamp grace interval, asymptotic stamps (rejected: softens the junk anchor), inside compensation (parked), partial fills
- Open: cross-window dynamics, staking-based fee caps, thin-market M fallbacks

</div>
